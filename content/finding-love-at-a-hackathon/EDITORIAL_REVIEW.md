# Finding Love at a Hackathon
## Editorial review

Completed November 13, 2026 against all ten canonical scripts and the regenerated schema 1.1 exports. The review covered the complete 100-shot dialogue sequence, action and camera descriptions, prop states, ending-to-opening handoffs and every prompt's audio instruction. Structural checks are recorded in [VALIDATION.md](VALIDATION.md).

| Review area | Finding and resulting decisions |
| --- | --- |
| First-demo prompt structure | Episode 01's ten existing `video_prompt` strings and the reusable template now map image 1 to [name] and image 2 to the adjacent media/lucy.png asset. Three explicit second ranges guide action/camera, with separate performance, continuity and full-soundscape directions. No focus-word onset or application timing machinery is introduced. Later episodes keep their existing prompt format. |
| Lucy reference alignment | The recurring romantic co-star and product-design partner is Lucy. Cast names, dialogue, overlays, prompts and templates agree with the supplied lucy.png reference. Her face and hair come from that reference while the hackathon wardrobe stays fixed. Both [name] and Lucy remain visible in all 100 shots. E02-S03 adds Lucy's visible reaction beside [name] without changing the date-check action, dialogue or focus word. Leo and Jules retain their identities and roles. |
| Story over vocabulary | Each word performs a dramatic job: safe follows a catch, proof challenges weak evidence, price exposes the cost of an offer, consent authorizes a specific revised build, and apart names the final separation. The bracket annotation is a production instruction, never spoken classroom dialogue. |
| Main-actor visibility | Every camera description names [name]. Phones, paper and the dead laptop stay below a recognizable face. The review-nook door moves Lucy into depth while [name] remains foreground for the full last shot. No prop-only, screen-only or reverse-angle shot is included. |
| Audio revision | Every prompt now asks for a normal full soundtrack, audible supporting lines and every non-target word in [name]'s line. Only the primary word is visibly mouthed without voice. Word-onset fields, scheduled dialogue windows and globally silent clips have been removed. Examples and templates use the same rule. |
| Breathing room | The complete dialogue read-through has 6–16 words per eight-second shot, including supporting responses. Learner lines are short ordinary phrases. Actions are concurrent with dialogue and limited to a small gesture, device action or short adjacent-zone move. There is space for eye reactions; there are no speeches requiring frantic delivery. |
| Immediate hooks | The first episode opens on a threatened physical loss. Later openings take up an active hold, evidence, erased name, career choice, blank device, removed pass, withdrawal, station conflict or power failure. Establishing an entire venue never consumes a shot. |
| Agency and accountability | Lucy chooses the partner, speaks for her own drafts, diagnoses the ownership flaw, decides to stop the unsafe build, acknowledges excluding [name], proposes a shared approval practice and opens the recovered file herself. [name] supports her without taking her devices or answering her audit. Leo has a legitimate station turn and remains a competitor after helping. |
| A fallible protagonist | [name] rushes a merge and admits it. Listening, protecting privacy and helping a rival make the impression; winning every technical argument does not. The later manual fallback is a design compromise, not a miraculous new distributed system. |
| Slow romance | Curiosity develops into respect, teasing, a chosen shared pause, an honest disagreement and practical repair. The first coffee invitation arrives only in E10 and receives an open answer. There is no kiss, declaration, exclusivity or completed relationship in this batch. |
| Causal cliffhangers | The ledger in ARC.md names each immediate payoff. The missing page appears promptly, the erased name is a stale unsubmitted draft, and Lucy owns the withdrawal rather than a concealed saboteur. The removed stage pass returns only after an actual witnessed test. E10's version-time discrepancy has a prompt E11 opening commitment. |
| Rules and stakes | Provisional entry, contributor registration, technical retry and authorship audit are distinct. The audit is seeded in E03 and retained in E04; it is not a rule invented after success. The early qualifier is not the final prize, leaving the remaining series somewhere to go. |
| Power and backup | The black battery is weak in E05. Desk power cycling is announced in E05 and again in E09. Lucy's charged silver laptop and current V2 USB are visible before the failure. The original folder remains in Lucy's custody. E10 opens the existing local file rather than rebuilding an app. |
| Signature clarity | E10's learner line asks Leo to sign as their witness. The form lists Lucy and [name] as authors; Leo does not sign either person's name or become a coauthor. His correction of the earlier release claim is distinct from the still-pending formal audit. |
| Readable graphics | Required short receipt, status and version labels are supplied as edit overlays. The story does not rely on generated tiny lettering or a real address/signature. Overlays coexist with the visible actor and do not create extra shots. |
| Season headroom | Only ten episodes are scripted. The 45-episode outline reserves the judged event, career consequences and chosen romantic direction for later phases. Optional 46–60 adds post-event consequences without revoking episode 45's intended resolution. |

This is a script-level review. No visual or audio render was generated, so output identity fidelity, target-only silent mouthing and soundtrack quality are not claimed as tested.
