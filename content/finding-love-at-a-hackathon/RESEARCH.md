# Finding Love at a Hackathon
## Format research and choices used

Researched online on November 13, 2026. These are craft references, not story sources. The characters, hackathon, project, dialogue and plot in this package are original.

[Sara Sioufi's StudioBinder guide, “How to Write a Micro Drama Script,” November 10, 2026](https://www.studiobinder.com/blog/micro-drama-script/) describes opening under pressure, using short dialogue that changes the situation, framing readable faces and small actions for phones, and planning episode endings as a causal chain. Applied here: the board tips immediately in E01-S01; a gold pass makes the career choice visible in E04; the withdrawal receipt changes a working relationship in E07; the dead laptop pays off the battery setup in E09. Dialogue asks, challenges, admits or chooses. It rarely explains information the viewer already has. Close two-shots and chest-height props preserve both the emotional response and [name]'s presence.

[Rebecca Norris Resnick's interview with screenwriter Lynn Grant Beck, published by the International Screenwriters' Association/Script Magazine, August 17, 2026](https://www.networkisa.org/script-magazine/view/true-indie-writing-for-verticals-interview-with-screenwriter-lynn-grant-beck) explains the one-to-two-minute scale, the narrow frame's preference for a limited cast, heightened emotion and cliffhanger endings. Applied here: a four-person recurring cast, three adjacent practical zones, and ten eight-second shots yield 80 seconds per episode. Most shots carry two people; four-person shots stack faces in depth. High emotion comes from fair credit, uncertain trust and choices with a cost, rather than elaborate action geography.

The structure used is immediate problem, escalation, emotional reversal, specific unresolved consequence. Each new episode addresses the previous ending in its opening shots. E02's missing page is produced in E03; E03's missing name is explained and restored in E04; E08's competing station turns are settled at the opening of E09. The cliffhanger ledger in [ARC.md](ARC.md) tracks the exact handoffs. A consequence can continue after its immediate question is answered: the stage pass removed in E06 is restored only after the witnessed test in E10.

The ten-shot constraint, 80-second baseline, one primary learning word per shot, visible [name] in every frame, 45-episode plan and optional expansion to 60 are this project's choices. They are not presented as universal industry rules. The current audio brief also comes from this project: generate normal dialogue, ambience, score and effects, with only [name]'s focus word silently mouthed. There is no word-onset or synchronization requirement.

Slow-burn romance uses a separate progression from the external crisis. Respecting a private notebook, returning individual credit, accepting a mistake, agreeing on shared decisions and helping a rival earn attention. E10 permits one tentative coffee invitation and leaves its answer open. The series does not need an early declaration to keep changing.
